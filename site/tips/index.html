Before you eat out in Tokyo

The questions travelers ask me most, answered short. Money, Suica, restaurant manners and the airport.

Cash or card?

Bring cash. Really.

Of the 298 places on this site, at least 117 say they are cash only (some others simply don't say). Small ramen, curry and soba shops are the most likely to be cash only.

How much?

173 of our 298 places are under ¥1,500 a person. A day of lunch, a sweet stop and dinner at everyday places is often ¥3,000–6,000. Carrying ¥10,000 in cash per day for food is a comfortable buffer. (That's my rule of thumb, not an official number.)

Where to get yen

7-Eleven and Japan Post ATMs take most foreign cards and have English menus. Check your bank's overseas fees before you go.

Suica & IC cards

iPhone

Download Welcome Suica Mobile (from JR East, iPhone and Apple Watch only). It lives in Apple Wallet, needs no deposit, works for 180 days and you top it up with Apple Pay.

Android

Mobile Suica on Android only works on phones made for Japan, so get a physical IC card at a station instead.

Where it pays

Trains and buses, convenience stores, and many chains and vending machines. Plenty of small restaurants still take cash only, so keep both.

At the restaurant

No tipping

Tipping isn't done in Japan. Service is included, and leaving money on the table may get you chased down the street with it.

Ticket machines (食券)

At many ramen, soba and curry shops you buy a ticket at the machine by the door first, then hand it to the staff. Many machines take only cash and older ones only take ¥1,000 notes.

Izakaya cover charge (お通し)

Most izakaya bring a small dish you didn't order, called otōshi. It's a seat charge, usually a few hundred yen, and it's normal.

Water is free

Tea or water comes free. Many places have a self-serve jug on the table or counter.

Pay at the register

You usually take your bill to the register by the door instead of paying at the table. Say "okaikei onegaishimasu" to ask for it.

Lines

Famous shops have lines. Join the end, wait outside, and order when the staff asks. Many close when they run out, so go early.

Before you fly

Visit Japan Web (optional)

Fill in immigration and customs online before you land and get one QR code for both. It's optional and only works at the 8 main airports; paper forms still work.

Tax-free shopping changed on Nov 1, 2026

You now pay tax at the shop and get it back after the customs check at the airport, within 90 days of buying. It needs ¥5,000 or more (before tax) at one shop in one day, and food or drinks you consume in Japan don't count.

Internet

Buy an eSIM before you fly (check your phone is unlocked) or rent pocket Wi-Fi at the airport. Google Maps and translation apps need data.

Allergies
